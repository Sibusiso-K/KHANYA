\begin{abstract}
\noindent Concentrators set grind, reagents and feed rate every shift, but the mineralogical evidence behind those settings, from automated mineralogy and fire assay, returns after the ore has been processed. This report describes KHANYA, a software system that identifies mineral phases in reflected-light micrographs of polished sections. It converts the segmentation into grain-level liberation measures and passes a gated recommendation to a simulated plant parameter over OPC~UA, with a person approving every change. A DeepLabV3--ResNet50 model trained on the public LumenStone~S2 dataset identifies pyrrhotite, chalcopyrite and pentlandite with intersection-over-union (IoU) of 0.8695, 0.5755 and 0.5468 on 12 held-out sections. The five-class mean IoU is 0.5725 (95\% CI 0.494--0.624) pooled over pixels and 0.4671 averaged per section; magnetite is never predicted. At the decision level, the shipped pipeline made no unsafe recommendation in 12 sections (one-sided 95\% upper bound 22.1\%), and all eight disagreements with expert-mask advice were requests for verification. A six-field scan takes 16.7--21.2\,s on a laptop CPU. An extension applies the same decision logic to a belt hyperspectral hardness prediction. On 146 public drill-core composites, a feed-rate policy built on a one-sided conformal bound gave a simulated throughput gain of +1.9\% (95\% CI +0.9 to +2.9\%), with overload frequency non-inferior within a pre-registered margin. All results are on public analogue ores, not South African ore, and the plant interaction is simulated. The proposed next step is a three-month laboratory shadow pilot with Mintek QEMSCAN data as ground truth.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} process mineralogy; reflected-light microscopy; semantic segmentation; liberation; advisory control; conformal prediction
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background and motivation}

South Africa produced about 71\% of the world's mined platinum in 2024 (120,000 of 170,000\,kg) \cite{usgs2025}. Its two main reefs recover very differently: Impala reported PGM recoveries of about 89\% on Merensky ore and about 79\% on UG2 \cite{impala2018}. UG2 carries about 30\% \cro{} against about 0.1\% for Merensky \cite{jones2005}, and its PGM grains are mostly finer than 10\um{} \cite{molefe2022}. The concentrate's chromite content is capped by the smelter, because \cro{} solubility in slag is only about 1.8\% \cite{eksteen2011}. That cap costs recovery. In Mintek testwork, UG2 concentrate reached about 430\,g/t at 87\% recovery with 2.9\% \cro{}, and higher grades and recoveries were possible if the \cro{} limit was relaxed \cite{jones2005}. Recovery changes of one or two percentage points are material: Valterra reported concentrator recovery gains of +1\,pp at Mototolo and +2\,pp at Amandelbult in 2025 \cite{valterra2025}. Comminution, which sets liberation, accounts for about half of a mine site's energy use \cite{napiermunn}.

The information needed to steer these trade-offs arrives late. Automated mineralogy (QEMSCAN, MLA, TIMA) measures phases, liberation and association, but it is a laboratory service: one Canadian list price was CAD\,1,500 per liberation analysis, plus CAD\,950 for preparation and mounting \cite{src2017}. Shift composites for fire assay are reported after the shift in which the ore was milled \verify{cite a primary source for typical 4E fire-assay turnaround, or delete this sentence}. The plant runs three shifts a day, so the shift decides without the mineralogy of the ore it is processing.

\subsection{Rationale: what existing instruments cannot tell the shift}

Plants already run many on-line sensors (Table~\ref{tab:instruments}). Each measures one property in one place, and none reports liberation on the shift's timescale. Advanced process control such as Mintek's MillStar and FloatStar stabilises the circuit, and FloatStar has been reported to raise recovery by up to 2.7\% over 400 days of on/off testing \cite{knights2012}. What is missing is an \emph{uncertainty-aware mineralogical input} to that layer: one that can be checked against the laboratory, and that refuses to advise when its evidence is thin. Recent Mintek work shows the value of mineralogy-constrained interpretation of flotation response \cite{moodley2026}. Our aim is to supply the mineralogical side of such interpretations faster and more cheaply, not to replace QEMSCAN.

\subsection{Contribution and scope}

The brief asks for a model that identifies at least three distinct mineral phases from provided image datasets, an accuracy report, and a demonstration of how the model's output can adjust plant parameters, in real time and integrated with existing controls. This report contributes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a segmentation model meeting the three-phase requirement, with per-class, per-section and decision-level accuracy, baselines and a like-for-like published comparison (Section~\ref{sec:results});
\item an end-to-end system that turns a micrograph into grain-level liberation measures, gates the recommendation, and drives a simulated plant parameter over OPC~UA after human approval (Sections~\ref{sec:methods}--\ref{sec:arch});
\item an extension of the same decision logic to a belt hyperspectral hardness prediction and a mill feed rate (Section~\ref{sec:belt}).
\end{enumerate}
Not claimed: performance on South African ore, any measured plant benefit, identification of chromite or silicates, or robustness to illumination change.

\subsection{Segmentation model}

The model is torchvision's DeepLabV3 with a ResNet-50 backbone \cite{chen2017,he2016}, initialised from the \texttt{COCO\_\allowbreak WITH\_\allowbreak VOC\_\allowbreak LABELS\_\allowbreak V1} weights and trained for five classes. Training used 8 epochs of 64 native-resolution 512\,px patches each, batch size 2, AdamW at $2\times10^{-4}$ and cross-entropy loss. Patch centres were sampled class-uniformly, so magnetite centres about 20\% of patches against its 1.84\% area share. The best validation patch mean IoU was 0.5384. The checkpoint under report has SHA-256 \sha{de7135a9\ldots} (168,313,587 bytes). The software refuses to command the simulator with any other checkpoint (Section~\ref{sec:gates}).

Inference has two modes. \emph{Full section} uses a sliding window of 512\,px tiles with 64\,px overlap, averaging logits. \emph{Quick} classifies six non-overlapping 512\,px fields on a fixed grid, covering 18.2\% of an S2 section. The six-field grid was fixed by the latency budget before its agreement with whole-section advice was measured.

\subsection{From mask to grains to advice}

Connected regions of the predicted mask are split into particles, with a boundary-topology refinement that separates touching grains. For S2, chalcopyrite and pentlandite are \emph{payload}, pyrrhotite is \emph{reject} and magnetite is \emph{oxide}. Each grain's payload fraction is its payload area divided by its classified area, giving three states: \emph{no valuable mineral} (fraction 0), \emph{locked} (between 0 and 0.5) and \emph{free} ($\geq 0.5$). The 50\% floor follows the steep fall in composite-particle flotation recovery below about half surface exposure \cite{liberation911}. The system also reports an area-weighted 2D association index, mineral contacts, composition and liberation by grain size. Liberation measured on 2D sections is biased high against volumetric liberation, so the advice treats it as an upper bound.

The rule-based advisor maps these measures to one of: \emph{grind finer}, \emph{continue at current setpoint}, or \emph{marginal: verify before acting}. Its liberation floor is sourced. Its other three thresholds (payload floor 0.003, reject ceiling 0.60, deleterious ceiling 0.05) are placeholders awaiting values from site metallurgists.

\subsection{Safety gates and the plant-parameter path}\label{sec:gates}

A recommendation reaches the plant simulator only if it passes every gate:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the running checkpoint is the approved one, identified by SHA-256;
\item the image is byte-identical to a validated held-out section (uploads are advisory only), and passes a colour-cast check that refuses greyscale and cool-cast images;
\item at least 9 payload-bearing particles were found (a provisional, conservative floor);
\item mean model confidence is at least 0.85 (uncalibrated softmax; a provisional floor).
\end{enumerate}
A person then triggers the change. The application programming interface (FastAPI) calls an OPC~UA server and client built on \texttt{asyncua} \cite{iec62541}, which set a simulated \texttt{regrind\_enabled} parameter. \emph{Grind finer} switches it on; \emph{continue} leaves it unchanged; any refusal holds the current setting and logs the reason. Each simulator session has its own plant state, and every event is tied to the result that produced it.

\subsection{Belt extension: hardness to feed rate}\label{sec:beltmethod}

A ball mill is usually limited by motor power. Bond's law gives the specific energy \cite{bond1961}
\begin{equation}
W = 10\,W_i\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{80}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{F_{80}}}\right)\ \text{kWh/t},
\end{equation}
so at a fixed grind target the achievable feed rate is proportional to $1/W_i$. Power, feed size and grind target cancel in every ratio below, so no plant parameter is assumed. A model predicts $W_i$ from each parcel's VNIR--SWIR spectrum. The feed rate is set from a one-sided 90\% split-conformal upper bound on $W_i$ \cite{lei2018}, not from the point prediction. Parcels flagged as out of distribution fall back to the conservative setting. An approximate CV+ bound \cite{barber2021} was tried first and failed its gate. The exact split-conformal bound, the plan's stated fallback, was then used, so the result is provisional (Section~\ref{sec:belt}).

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}

All segmentation metrics use the publisher's 12 held-out S2 sections, never used for training or checkpoint selection. Per-class IoU is $\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}+\mathrm{FN})$. We report two conventions: \emph{pooled} (one confusion matrix over all 103,795,344 test pixels) and \emph{per section} (the mean of each section's mean IoU). Confidence intervals are percentile bootstraps over sections (2,000 resamples, seed 42), because a section, not a pixel, is the independent unit. Baselines are majority class and colour-only nearest centroid. The published comparison uses the void-border protocol, which excludes pixels within 5\,px of a labelled boundary.

Decision-level agreement runs the advisor twice per section, once on the model's mask and once on the expert mask. A disagreement is \emph{unsafe} if the model says \emph{continue} where the expert-mask advice does not, or gives any non-hedged advice where the expert mask says \emph{grind finer}. It is \emph{conservative} if the model acts where it need not, and \emph{flagged} if it hedges to verification. This measures consistency with the same rules applied to expert labels, not metallurgical correctness.

The belt model is evaluated out of fold, grouped, against its strongest baseline, with gates fixed in a pre-registration committed before each run.

\section{System architecture}\label{sec:arch}

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the system. The user interface is React with TypeScript; the production bundle is committed, so it runs offline. The FastAPI service runs PyTorch inference on the CPU, loads and warms the model at start-up, and disables analysis until the model is ready. During a scan it publishes provisional phase fractions computed only over fields already classified. A grain inspector decodes a 24-bit grain-identity image in the browser, so a tap on a grain shows its composition, size and liberation state. Reports are bound to the checkpoint's SHA-256 and show that checkpoint's own accuracy. In local mode the service accepts only loopback connections and makes no external network requests. An optional assistant may route questions, but no language model computes any measured or control value.

The belt layer (REEFPRINT Live) has a secure server mode with role-based access, scrypt password hashing, server-side sessions, CSRF protection and rate limits. It keeps an append-only decision ledger with Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65 signed checkpoints \cite{fips204}, and passed 17 of 17 security tests locally; it has not been deployed publicly. The public demonstration is a static site whose decision record is an unsigned browser sandbox.

\textbf{Software verification.} The workbench has 180 pytest functions in 27 files and 7 browser tests (Playwright) in 5 specifications. Its four continuous-integration checks (unit tests, offline-bundle match, local API tests and secret scanning) passed on the reported revision. The belt branch has 328 pytest functions; static analysis (Bandit) reported 0 issues, and a dependency audit found 9 known vulnerabilities, all fixed by upgrading \texttt{cryptography}.

\subsection{Against baselines and published work}

Table~\ref{tab:compare} places the model against trivial baselines and against two other checkpoints from the same project. Colour alone reaches 0.3948, so the network adds most where colour fails, between the sulphides. A second run of the identical training recipe (\sha{fb78727}) scored 0.4543, because the patch sampler is unseeded. The figures in this report therefore describe this checkpoint, not the method. A retrained candidate (\sha{42646cfa}) scores 0.6320 and detects magnetite (IoU 0.2477; recall 0.893, precision 0.255). But it loses pyrrhotite (0.8183) and pentlandite (0.4460), the phases that matter most for PGM deportment, so it is not deployed.

On LumenStone~S1, using the exact 16 test images of the published ResUNet benchmark, our separate S1 checkpoint scores 0.7224 against a published 0.8506 (void-border), a gap of $-0.1282$ \cite{resunet}. Tennantite and galena, grey phases close in reflectance to their neighbours, account for 75.7\% of the gap (Appendix~\ref{app:s1}). Published work detects magnetite on this modality (IoU 0.650) \cite{korshunov2025}, so our zero is a limit of this model's training budget, not of reflected light.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Engineering significance}

A segmentation score answers how well pixels are labelled. The system is built to answer a different question: what the shift should do with this ore, and how sure it can be. Two levers are covered. The microscope layer gives the metallurgist liberation evidence for a grind or regrind decision. The belt layer gives the control room a hardness bound for the mill feed rate. In both, the system is an advisory input that sits beside existing control, such as Mintek's MillStar and FloatStar, inside limits the site sets. It is not a controller.

Two design choices carry most of the safety argument. First, topology refinement did not reduce disagreement with expert-mask advice, but it turned every confident error into a request for verification. Second, when the system abstains or the operator does not respond, the site's conservative setting applies, never the last value, because the last value may itself be wrong. The original proposal said the opposite; we reversed it after review.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No South African ore has been evaluated.} S2 is Norilsk ore, an analogue for the sulphide assemblage only, and the belt data are Chilean Cu--Mo.
\item The 12 held-out sections were reused throughout development and may not be independent specimens.
\item Magnetite is never detected; chromite and the silicates were never trained on.
\item The advice is sensitive to illumination (8 of 12 sections changed under a realistic exposure shift).
\item The checkpoint reproduces bit-for-bit, but the training recipe does not.
\item Decision-level results measure consistency with a rule set whose thresholds are partly unsourced.
\item Liberation is measured in 2D and is an upper bound on volumetric liberation. In Quick mode most grains are cut by field borders (19 of 24 on test\_11), and pixel sizes are uncalibrated.
\item Latency is CPU-only with small $n$ and excludes section preparation.
\item The plant interaction is a simulation. No claim is made about reagent use, recovery or turnaround at a plant.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Indicative value}

No plant benefit has been measured, so value is shown as a worked example with labelled assumptions (Appendix~\ref{app:assumptions}). One percentage point of recovery is worth
\begin{equation}
\Delta V = \frac{T\,g}{31.1035}\times 0.01 \times p \times P
\end{equation}
per year, where $T$ is tonnes milled, $g$ the 4E head grade in g/t, $p$ the payability and $P$ the net price per ounce. At a Zondereinde-sized plant ($T$ = 2,247,881\,t/yr, $g$ = 4.72\,g/t \verify{read both from Northam's F2025 annual report}), with $p$ = 0.75/0.85/0.95 and $P$ = R25,000/R32,611/R47,156 per ounce, this gives R64.0\,M, R94.6\,M and R152.8\,M per year. The base price is Valterra's 2025 realised basket \cite{valterra2025}, used as a proxy. The middle case is about US\$5.4\,M per point. Against an assumed belt installation of US\$0.5--3.0\,M with US\$0.1--0.6\,M a year in running costs (five years, 10\%), the break-even recovery gain is 0.043--0.259\,pp. These are scenarios, not forecasts; the pilot exists to measure the real number.

\section{Future work, ask and roadmap}

The next step is a three-month laboratory shadow pilot with Mintek's QEMSCAN as ground truth (Fig.~\ref{fig:roadmap}). It addresses the largest limitation, no South African ore, at the lowest cost. Each later phase starts only when the gate before it is passed. The technical work, each item tied to a limitation above, is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item retrain and validate on QEMSCAN-labelled UG2 and Merensky sections, with locality-disjoint test sets, adding chromite and the silicates;
\item promote a magnetite-capable model only if it keeps the current pyrrhotite and pentlandite IoU;
\item seed the patch sampler and report a multi-seed mean with its spread;
\item add colour normalisation and an exposure gate before inference;
\item set the three placeholder advisor thresholds with Mintek metallurgists;
\item use the liberation classes of Moodley et al.\ \cite{moodley2026} to suggest the next timed-concentrate test, the paper's own stated future work;
\item test the belt layer on Bushveld core, with the wavelength-calibration gate in hardware;
\item measure GPU and edge latency, reporting p95 over at least 30 runs.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{The ask.} Three partners are needed: a producer with a laboratory and a belt; Mintek as the truth laboratory and the route into its process-control products; and development funding \verify{confirm the named funder before citing}.

\section{Conclusions}

A model trained on public reflected-light micrographs identifies three sulphide phases with IoU 0.5468--0.8695 on held-out sections. That meets the brief's three-phase requirement, with magnetite as a stated failure. Built into an end-to-end system with explicit gates, its recommendations reached a simulated plant parameter only after human approval. On 12 held-out sections it made no unsafe recommendation, although 12 sections bound the unsafe rate only to below 22.1\%. The same decision logic applied to a belt hardness bound gave a provisional simulated throughput gain of +1.9\%. These results hold for public analogue ores and a simulated plant. Whether they transfer to South African ore is the question a QEMSCAN-labelled laboratory pilot is designed to answer.

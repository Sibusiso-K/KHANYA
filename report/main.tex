\begin{abstract}
\noindent South Africa mined about 71\% of the world's platinum in 2025, yet its concentrators still set mill feed, grind and reagents before the laboratory can say what was in the ore. REEFPRINT/KHANYA reads the ore at three speeds and turns what it reads into one decision that a person approves. On the belt, a hyperspectral camera feeds a model that predicts the ore's grinding hardness (Bond work index) with a safety upper bound, in 92\,ms per parcel on an ordinary processor. At the output of Northam's Zondereinde mine, each 1\% more recovered metal is worth R109--153\,million a year in revenue, so a one-belt pilot costing R8--49\,million installed breaks even at 0.02--0.21\% more recovered metal. We ask the Technology Innovation Agency, Mintek and one producer for a nine-month pilot: one belt, one truth laboratory, with fees paid only on measured value.

\section{Introduction}

As graduate engineers working on Mintek's Problem 3, our responsibility is to give a concentrator's shift team a reliable reading of its ore before that ore reaches the mill, and to turn the reading into a plant setting that a person can approve. This report sets out what we built, how we tested it, what it would cost and earn, and how a funded pilot would prove it on South African ore. It is written for metallurgists, mineralogists, process engineers and the people who fund their technology, so the technical detail is limited to what a decision needs.

\subsection{Problem statement}

The brief states the problem plainly: ``without real-time feedback, processing plants cannot adjust to changes in ore quality, leading to inefficient chemical usage and lower mineral yields'' \cite{brief}. Ore reaches the mill within minutes of leaving the belt, whereas assays and automated mineralogy are batch laboratory work. Mintek's own Minerals Processing and Characterisation Cluster runs QEMSCAN \cite{moodley2026}, and a commercial laboratory describes mineralogy as ``not an overnight assay service'' \cite{als}. Consequently, each shift sets the mill and the flotation circuit for ore whose mineralogy it has not yet seen.

The cost of deciding blind falls most heavily on South Africa. Figure~\ref{fig:world} illustrates world PGM mine production in 2025, highlighting that South Africa mined an estimated 120 of the world's 170 tonnes of platinum (about 71\%) and 70 of 190 tonnes of palladium. South Africa also holds 63,000 of the world's more than 76,000 tonnes of PGM reserves, mostly in the Bushveld Complex \cite{usgs2026}. The same source attributes the 9\% fall in South African PGM output in 2025 to lower palladium prices, higher deep-level mining costs and ongoing disruptions to the electricity supply. Zimbabwe, the third-largest platinum producer, mines the same family of ores on the Great Dyke.

Recovery is where the money is. UG2 ore carries about 30\% \cro{} against about 0.1\% for Merensky ore, and the smelter caps the chromite in concentrate. Mintek testwork made UG2 concentrate at 87\% recovery with 2.9\% \cro{}, and recoveries above 90\% were possible only if the \cro{} limit was relaxed \cite{jones2005}. Producers report recovery moves of this size to investors: Valterra raised concentrator recoveries by 1.2--1.7 percentage points at Mototolo and Amandelbult in 2025 \cite{valterra2025}. Comminution, which sets both liberation and mill throughput, used 52\% of site electricity in a benchmark of mines \cite{ballantyne2012}, on a grid that is itself a constraint.

Thus, the problem is to read the ore before the mill, state how uncertain the reading is, and turn it into a safe, approved setting at a cost a producer can recover.

\subsection{Our solution: three speeds, one decision}

REEFPRINT/KHANYA reads the ore at three speeds and turns all three into one decision (Section~\ref{sec:arch}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{the belt} (REEFPRINT), in seconds: a hyperspectral camera above the mill-feed conveyor feeds a model that predicts each parcel's grinding hardness with an upper bound, and Bond's law turns the bound into a mill feed-rate proposal;
\item \textbf{the laboratory} (KHANYA), within the shift: a microscope model identifies the sulphide minerals and how well they are liberated, which is the brief's three-phase deliverable;
\item \textbf{the decision}, within minutes: a metallurgist or controller approves each proposal before the ore reaches the mill, or the site's conservative setting applies, and every step is signed and recorded.
\end{enumerate}
It is important to note what is not claimed: performance on South African ore, a measured plant benefit, mineral identification by the belt camera, or identification of chromite and the silicates by the microscope.

\section{How the system works}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Data}

No South African belt scans or labelled Bushveld micrographs were available, so every model was trained and tested on public data (Table~\ref{tab:data}). HIDSAG supplies hyperspectral scans of Chilean porphyry copper--molybdenum drill core with laboratory Bond work-index values \cite{ehrenfeld2023}. LumenStone~S2, a Norilsk nickel--copper sulphide ore \cite{korshunov2025}, shares the mineral assemblage of Bushveld ores but not their abundance: sulphides cover 62.8\% of its sections by area, whereas Merensky ore contains up to about 3\% base-metal sulphides and UG2 less \cite{jones2005}. Nevertheless, it is the right teacher for the phases, because the base-metal sulphides in UG2 are predominantly pentlandite, chalcopyrite, pyrrhotite and pyrite \cite{jones2005}.

\subsection{The belt layer: hardness from the infrared}\label{sec:beltmethod}

\emph{Sensor (design).} The design uses a short-wave infrared line scanner such as the Specim SX25 (960--2500\,nm, 640 pixels across, up to 162 lines a second) with a visible--near-infrared companion camera \cite{specim}. On a 1.2\,m belt moving at 2\,m/s it records a line every 12.3\,mm with 1.88\,mm pixels. It therefore averages over parcels of ore; it does not map individual particles.

\emph{Model.} Each parcel's spectrum is reduced to features from the infrared absorption regions of clay, mica, chlorite and carbonate minerals (Fig.~\ref{fig:hsi}), and a model predicts the Bond work index. Results are reported only for samples the model never saw (out of fold), and a result counted only if it beat simple baselines under pass marks written down before each run.

\emph{Bound and gate.} The plant setting uses an upper bound that the true hardness should stay below 9 times out of 10 (a one-sided 90\% split-conformal bound \cite{lei2018}), not the best guess. A more data-efficient bound \cite{barber2021} was tried first and failed its pass mark, so the exact bound that the plan named as its fallback is used, and the result is provisional. A second check flags parcels unlike anything in training; flagged parcels receive the conservative setting.

\subsection{The decision layer}\label{sec:decision}

The decision layer turns the hardness bound into a mill setting that a person can approve. Ball mills are usually limited by motor power, so the tonnes a mill can grind depend on how hard the ore is. Bond's law gives the specific grinding energy \cite{bond1961}
\begin{equation}
W = 10\,W_i\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{80}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{F_{80}}}\right)\ \text{kWh/t},
\end{equation}
where $W_i$ is the work index and $F_{80}$ and $P_{80}$ are the feed and product sizes (\textmu m) passed by 80\% of the material. At a fixed grind target the feed rate the mill can carry is proportional to $1/W_i$, so the proposal is
\begin{equation}
F_{\text{proposed}} = F_{\text{design}} \times \frac{W_{i,\text{design}}}{W_{i,\text{bound}}}.
\end{equation}
Power, feed size and grind target cancel, so no plant parameter has to be assumed. For example, a design hardness of 16.6\,kWh/t and a bound of 16.9\,kWh/t give 98.2\% of design feed. The proposal must sit inside an operating envelope set by the site's metallurgist. The demonstration envelope is 85--110\% of design feed, with 100\% as the conservative setting; feed may be cut by 10\% but raised by only 3\% per parcel.

A person may approve, modify within the envelope, reject or escalate. If nobody approves before the parcel reaches the mill (an assumed 90\,s), or the parcel is flagged, or the data are stale, the conservative setting applies. The system never holds the last value, because the last value may itself be wrong. Every event is written to a tamper-evident, hash-chained record with conventional (Ed25519) and post-quantum (ML-DSA-65 \cite{fips204}) signatures. The microscope's advice reaches a simulated regrind setting over OPC~UA, the industrial data-exchange standard \cite{opcua}; the same interface is designed for the feed rate.

\subsection{The laboratory layer: minerals under the microscope}\label{sec:micromethod}

The microscope model is a standard image-segmentation network (DeepLabV3 with a ResNet-50 backbone \cite{chen2017,he2016}) that labels every pixel of a reflected-light image as resin, pyrrhotite, chalcopyrite, pentlandite or magnetite. The approved checkpoint (SHA-256 \sha{de7135a9\ldots}) is the only one allowed to send advice to the simulator. A \emph{Quick} scan classifies six 512-pixel fields of a section; a \emph{full} scan covers the whole section.

Connected regions of the mask are split into grains. Chalcopyrite and pentlandite are the valuable (payload) phases, and pyrrhotite is rejected. A grain is \emph{free} if at least half its area is payload and \emph{locked} otherwise; the 50\% line is the boundary between low and high middlings in the liberation classes used by Mintek's characterisation group \cite{moodley2026}. A rule-based advisor turns these measures into \emph{grind finer}, \emph{continue} or \emph{verify before acting}, but only if the checkpoint is approved, the image passes a colour check, at least 9 payload-bearing grains were found and mean confidence is at least 0.85. Three of its four thresholds are placeholders for site metallurgists to set.

\subsection{How we tested it}

Belt models were scored out of fold against the strongest simple baseline (the fold mean, the fold median and a colour-only model), with pass marks committed to the repository before each run. The microscope was scored on the publisher's 12 held-out S2 sections, never used for training or model selection. Its accuracy is the intersection over union (IoU: the overlap between predicted and expert-labelled areas, from 0 to 1), pooled over 103.8 million pixels and averaged per section, with bootstrap confidence intervals over sections. Decision-level agreement runs the advisor on the model's mask and on the expert's mask; a disagreement is \emph{unsafe} if the model gives a confident instruction that the expert mask does not support, for example \emph{continue} where the expert mask says \emph{grind finer}. A request to verify is safe.

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} illustrates the system, highlighting that both layers pass the same safety gates and the same human decision. The belt monitor (REEFPRINT Live) runs the hardness model, the decision view and the signed record. The microscope workbench (KHANYA) is a web application (React) on a Python service (FastAPI and PyTorch) that runs on an ordinary laptop processor. In local mode it accepts connections only from the same machine and makes no external requests, so a network outage at the plant does not stop the advice. No language model computes a measured value or a control value.

Speeds were measured on a laptop processor: 92\,ms per belt parcel (median, four cores); 16.7--21.2\,s per six-field microscope scan on mains power (5 runs) and 30.8\,s on battery; 162\,s for a full section. The 90\,s approval window is an assumed belt-to-mill transit time. Three environments exist, and each claim names its environment: a static public demonstration of the belt monitor on precomputed data with an unsigned record; a secure server (role-based access, password hashing, request forgery protection, rate limits and the signed record) that passed 17 of 17 security tests locally and is not deployed; and the microscope workbench, which ran locally. The workbench has 180 unit-test functions and 7 browser tests, with four continuous-integration checks passing, and the belt software has 328 unit-test functions; static security analysis reported no issues after a dependency upgrade.

Figure~\ref{fig:decision} shows the decision for the same parcel as the operator sees it. Figure~\ref{fig:policies} illustrates four feed-rate policies replayed over all 146 composites, highlighting the trade-off between throughput and overloads. Setting the feed from the bound gave a simulated throughput gain of +1.9\% (95\% CI +0.9 to +2.9\%), and overloaded parcels fell from 8.9\% to 6.8\%; the one-sided 95\% upper bound on the change (+1.4 points) is inside the +3-point margin set before the test. Perfect knowledge would add 14.0\%, so the bound captures about one-seventh of what is available, and better hardness models have room to add value.

\section{Validation against literature and limitations}

\subsection{Validation against literature}

The results align with published work and with what producers already report. Notole et al.\ \cite{notole2025} validated hyperspectral imaging and portable XRF against reference methods on Merensky Reef drill core scanned at the Council for Geoscience in Pretoria, so the sensing layer has been shown to work on Bushveld material, although not yet for hardness. Our 50\% free/locked line sits on the boundary between low and high middlings in Mintek's liberation classes \cite{moodley2026}, and the three sulphides the microscope identifies are the main base-metal sulphides of UG2 ore \cite{jones2005}. On the plant side, the effects we test for are smaller than those already reported. Mintek's AI optimisation raised a lead--zinc concentrator's throughput by 0.5\%, worth over US\$2 million (about \Rm{33}) a year \cite{mintek2025}, and Valterra's recovery gains of 1.2--1.7 percentage points \cite{valterra2025} are many times our break-even (Section~\ref{sec:finance}).

Four ideas were tested and withdrawn, and they mark where the method stops. The belt spectra did not predict QEMSCAN mineralogy once size fraction and process line were accounted for (no skill on any of 33 minerals). Belt chemistry routed Bushveld PGE ore worse than the mine plan's seam label (balanced accuracy 0.79 against 0.82). The belt did not predict recovery, lime or pH better than a baseline. Plant tags never forecast concentrate silica an hour ahead better than the last laboratory assay.

\subsection{Limitations}

The following limitations apply: (a) no South African ore has been evaluated, because the belt data are Chilean copper core and the microscope data are Norilsk ore; (b) the belt result is simulated on 146 composites without time order, so it is provisional until a pilot measures it; (c) the belt camera predicts hardness and does not identify minerals, while the microscope has not been trained on chromite or the silicates and never detects magnetite; (d) the 12 held-out sections were reused during development, and the training recipe is not exactly reproducible; (e) the microscope's advice is sensitive to illumination, three advisor thresholds are unsourced, and liberation is measured in 2D, which overstates it; and (f) latency is measured on one laptop with small samples, and the plant is a simulator. Compared with the abstract Mintek accepted, three things changed: the five Bushveld phases became three sulphides on an analogue ore, because no labelled Bushveld images existed; the QEMSCAN ``teacher'' became the pilot's truth-laboratory stream; and the fallback was reversed from holding the last setpoint to applying the conservative setting.

\subsection{Value proposition and business model}

Our value proposition is simple: the plant gets a reading of its ore before the mill, with a stated bound, and pays only for value that an independent party has measured. The buyer is the concentrator manager or general manager for metallurgy at a South African PGM producer, the person who owns recovery, concentrate quality and mill throughput. Producers such as Valterra, Implats, Sibanye-Stillwater, Northam and Tharisa are named as market segments, not customers. Figure~\ref{fig:business} illustrates how money and value flow between the partners, highlighting that the producer's fee is tied to measured value. The Technology Innovation Agency (TIA), the national agency that funds technology development \cite{tia}, is the proposed bridge funder.

The revenue model has four streams: a paid laboratory pilot; a monthly licence for the laboratory software; recovery of the belt pilot's cost; and, after a successful pilot, at most 20\% of the measured net value per concentrator per year. The model is justified for four reasons, each drawn from Mintek's own published work \cite{mintek2025}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{It creates more truth-laboratory work, not less.} The models need QEMSCAN composites and Bond tests to retrain and audit them, and Mintek runs QEMSCAN \cite{moodley2026}. Mintek already characterises drill core for chromite and iron-ore washability and applies ore characterisation to flotation reagent selection for PGMs and base-metal sulphides.
\item \textbf{It is a feed-forward input to Mintek's process control.} Mintek's AI-driven optimisation raised a concentrator's throughput by 0.5\%, worth over US\$2 million a year; a hardness bound that arrives before the ore is the kind of input such optimisation can use.
\item \textbf{Mintek has already proven value-linked pricing.} Its benefit-sharing model with Hindustan Zinc in India links revenue to system performance and has generated over \Rm{35} since 2021.
\item \textbf{It has a route beyond South Africa.} Mintek's products and services are used in over 60 countries, its instruments group operates in 42, and it works through distributors including Molycop and Mercantil. Its footprint includes Zimbabwe, Zambia, the DRC and Chile, where the belt model was developed.
\end{enumerate}
The timing also favours a pilot now: South African PGM output fell 9\% in 2025 \cite{usgs2026}, while Valterra's realised basket price rose from R32,611 per ounce in 2025 to R45,993 in the first half of 2026 \cite{valterra2025,valterra2026}, so each ounce recovered is worth 41\% more.

\subsection{Financial methodology}\label{sec:finance}

No plant benefit has been measured, so the case is a worked example with stated assumptions: (a) annual output is that of Northam's Zondereinde mine, 333,050 equivalent refined 4E ounces in F2026 \cite{northam2026}; (b) metal prices are Valterra's realised basket prices of R32,611 per ounce (2025) and R45,993 (first half of 2026) \cite{valterra2025,valterra2026}, used as proxies; (c) value is revenue before incremental processing and refining costs, so it overstates profit; (d) costs are the ranges in Table~\ref{tab:pricing}; and (e) the pilot equipment has a 5-year life at a 10\% discount rate.

The annual value of an uplift $u$ in recovered metal is
\begin{equation}
V = u \times Q \times P ,
\end{equation}
where $Q$ is annual output in ounces and $P$ the price per ounce. For $u$ = 1\%,
\begin{equation}
V = 0.01 \times 333{,}050 \times (\text{R}32{,}611 \text{ to } \text{R}45{,}993),
\end{equation}
which is equivalent to R108.6--153.2\,million a year. The installed cost is spread over the equipment's life with the capital recovery factor
\begin{equation}
\text{CRF} = \frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n-1} = 0.2638 \quad (r = 10\%,\ n = 5),
\end{equation}
so the annualised cost is
\begin{equation}
C = \text{CRF} \times C_{\text{installed}} + C_{\text{running}},
\end{equation}
which is equivalent to \Rm{3.8} a year (low), \Rm{11.4} (middle) and \Rm{22.8} (high), or \Rm{25.4} for the high case with contingency. The break-even uplift is then
\begin{equation}
u_{\text{BE}} = \frac{C}{Q \times P},
\end{equation}
which gives 0.02\% in the best case (low cost, 2026 price) and 0.21\% in the worst (high cost, 2025 price), or 0.23\% with contingency. Figure~\ref{fig:breakeven} illustrates the break-even, highlighting that every crossing lies below a quarter of a per cent.

\section{Conclusions and recommendation}

A hyperspectral camera on the belt can predict grinding hardness well enough to steer a mill: on 146 public drill-core composites the model explained 48\% of the variance in Bond work index, more than twice what colour alone explains, and a feed-rate policy built on its upper bound gave a provisional simulated throughput gain of 1.9\% without more overloads. A microscope model identified the three main base-metal sulphides of UG2 ore on held-out sections, with magnetite as a stated failure, and gave no unsafe advice on 12 sections. Both feed one decision that a person approves and a signed record keeps. These results hold for public analogue ores and a simulated plant.

The financial case does not depend on large effects. At Zondereinde's output a one-belt pilot breaks even at 0.02--0.21\% more recovered metal, a small fraction of the recovery moves producers already report, and the producer pays a share of value only once an independent party has measured it. We therefore recommend that TIA, Mintek and one PGM producer approve a nine-month pilot: one belt and one truth laboratory, with go/no-go gates at months 2, 5 and 9. Whether the results transfer to South African ore is exactly the question that pilot is designed to answer.
